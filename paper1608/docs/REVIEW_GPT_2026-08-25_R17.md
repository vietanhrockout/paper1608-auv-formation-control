## Verdict

**REQUEST CHANGES — Fig. 4 remains an invalid cost-to-go reconstruction.**

The negative curves are not a plotting or indexing error. The plot correctly
recomputes the implemented critic output

\[
\hat C_i(t)=\hat W_{ci}(t)^T\theta_c(\chi_i(t)).
\]

The problem is upstream: the unconstrained linear RBF critic learns negative
weights during the initial tracking transient. This makes \(\hat C_i<0\) even
though the true Eq. (15) cost-to-go is non-negative by construction. The
current evidence does **not** support changing the sign of Eq. (19), negating
the plotted series, or plotting `abs(Chat)`. Those would hide the failed value
estimate rather than repair or explain it.

The latest Claude R16 follow-up branch changes status documents, regression
binding, diagnostic messages, and one stale RHS comment. It does not change
`critic_basis.m`, `bellman_error.m`, `critic_update.m`, `nn_config.m`, or the
Fig. 4 quantity. This finding therefore applies to both inspected tips.

## 1. Mathematical consistency check

Paper Eq. (15) defines

\[
C(t)=\int_t^\infty \exp\!\left(\frac{t-r_0}{\lambda}\right)r(r_0)\,dr_0,
\qquad \lambda>0,
\]

and Eq. (16), using the supervisor-confirmed physically applied saturated
input, is

\[
r(t)=\chi(t)^TB\chi(t)+\tau_{act}(t)^TR\tau_{act}(t),
\qquad B\succ0,\ R\succ0.
\]

Therefore

\[
r(t)\ge 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(t)\ge0.
\]

Differentiating Eq. (15) gives

\[
\dot C(t)=-r(t)+\frac{1}{\lambda}C(t),
\]

or equivalently

\[
r(t)-\frac{1}{\lambda}C(t)+\dot C(t)=0.
\]

This is the sign structure used by paper Eq. (17) and by
`nn/bellman_error.m`. I found no simple Eq. (17)–(19) sign-transcription bug in
the implementation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\hat C &= \hat W_c^T\theta,\\
\Phi &= -\frac{1}{\lambda}\theta+\dot\theta,\\
c_e &= r+\hat W_c^T\Phi,\\
\dot{\hat W}_c &= -\lambda_c c_e\Phi.
\end{aligned}
\]

The implementation is paper-literal here. Paper-literal, however, does not
imply that the learned approximation is a valid non-negative value function.

## 2. Direct cause of the sign reversal

For the implemented critic,

\[
\dot{\hat C}
=\dot{\hat W}_c^T\theta+\hat W_c^T\dot\theta
=-\lambda_c c_e\Phi^T\theta+\hat W_c^T\dot\theta.
\]

The repository initializes every critic weight to zero. Thus, exactly at
initialization,

\[
\hat C(0)=0,\qquad c_e(0)=r(0)>0,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\dot{\hat C}(0)=-\lambda_c r(0)\Phi(0)^T\theta(0)}.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\Phi^T\theta
=-\frac{\|\theta\|^2}{\lambda}+\theta^T\dot\theta.
\]

During the rapid initial formation transient, the basis-motion term
\(\theta^T\dot\theta\) becomes larger than
\(\|\theta\|^2/\lambda\). Once \(\Phi^T\theta>0\), the positive immediate
cost makes \(\dot{\hat C}<0\) at zero/small weights. This is the observed sign
reversal.

Independent reconstruction from
`artifacts/validation/phase_b3_result_t15.mat` gives:

| Vehicle | \(\Phi^T\theta(0)\) | first \(\Phi^T\theta>0\) | first \(\hat C<0\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| AUV0 | \(-1.3\times10^{-7}\) | 0.0298 s | 0.0596 s |
| AUV1 | \(-7.0043\times10^{-4}\) | 0.0447 s | 0.0894 s |
| AUV2 | \(-2.5050\times10^{-2}\) | 0.0447 s | 0.0894 s |

The first saved AUV2 samples expose the mechanism directly:

| \(t\) (s) | \(\hat C_2\) | \(\Phi_2^T\theta_2\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0000 | 0.000000 | -0.025050 |
| 0.0149 | +0.009958 | -0.016345 |
| 0.0298 | +0.015780 | -0.007842 |
| 0.0447 | +0.017559 | +0.000516 |
| 0.0596 | +0.015355 | +0.008763 |
| 0.0745 | +0.009213 | +0.016922 |
| 0.0894 | -0.000840 | +0.025012 |

The 100 s production artifact stores only 1,003 samples and misses most of
this sub-0.1 s positive excursion, which is why the plotted series appears
non-positive from the start. This is a sampling/visibility effect, not the
cause of the negative trajectory.

## 3. Why the learned output remains negative

All Gaussian critic basis activations are strictly positive. At 100 s, every
one of the 15 final critic-weight components is negative for all three AUVs:

| Vehicle | min final weight | max final weight | mean final weight | all 15 negative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| AUV0 | -7.728631 | -0.034550 | -1.993178 | yes |
| AUV1 | -6.541099 | -0.035714 | -1.678634 | yes |
| AUV2 | -4.663035 | -0.050359 | -1.222486 | yes |

Consequently \(\hat W_c^T\theta_c<0\). Paper Eq. (20) and
`nn/projection_operator.m` constrain only the weight norm. They impose no
non-negativity constraint on the weights or on \(\hat C\). In the corrected
Phase-C run the maximum observed norm is only 14.3324, far inside the assumed
\(\delta_c=100\), so projection is inactive as an explanation for the sign.

The negative value is also not a true steady solution of the literal Bellman
update. When the trajectory becomes slow,
\(\dot\theta\approx0\) and \(\Phi\approx-\theta/\lambda\). If \(\hat C<0\),

\[
c_e\approx r-\frac{\hat C}{\lambda}>0,
\]

so the update slowly moves the critic back toward positive values. The raw
artifact confirms this recovery:

| Vehicle | \(\hat C(50\,s)\) | \(\hat C(100\,s)\) | linear slope, 50–100 s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| AUV0 | -6.3202 | -5.7916 | +0.010465/s |
| AUV1 | -5.4817 | -5.0234 | +0.009315/s |
| AUV2 | -4.3039 | -3.9543 | +0.006987/s |

Each curve changes by approximately 8% over the last 50 s. Therefore the
current `plot_fig4_cost_to_go.m` wording "THREE DISTINCT, STABLE plateaus" is
not supported by its own data. These are slowly recovering negative tails,
not demonstrated converged plateaus.

## 4. Independent positive-return check

As a lower-bound check, I backward-integrated only the state term
\(\chi^T\chi\) over the committed interval \([0,100]\) using the repository's
assumed \(B=I\):

\[
C_{state,[0,100]}(t)
=\int_t^{100}e^{-(s-t)/\lambda}\chi(s)^T\chi(s)\,ds.
\]

This omits both the non-negative applied-control term and all non-negative
cost after 100 s. At \(t=0\) it is already:

| Vehicle | \(\chi(0)^T\chi(0)\) | truncated state-only return |
|---|---:|---:|
| AUV0 | 328 | 612.7187 |
| AUV1 | 157 | 256.9166 |
| AUV2 | 101 | 109.0968 |

Thus the learned value beginning at zero and subsequently becoming negative
cannot be Eq. (15)'s return for the executed trajectory. This conclusion does
not depend on the omitted control term.

## 5. Paper/reproduction gaps exposed by Fig. 4

### [P1] The paper's critic does not preserve a property of its own target

Eqs. (15)–(16) guarantee \(C\ge0\), while the unconstrained linear
approximation in Eq. (14) and norm-only projection in Eq. (20) allow
\(\hat C<0\). The paper gives no positivity-preserving parameterization,
boundary condition, or theorem proving \(\hat C\ge0\).

### [P1] The claimed optimal/value convergence is not established

The stability section proves boundedness of NN weights and practical
predefined-time convergence of closed-loop errors. It does not establish
persistent excitation, \(c_e\to0\), \(\hat C\to C\), convergence to the HJB
solution, or policy optimality. Therefore paper statements that the critic
"derives minimum-cost torque inputs" and that Fig. 4 validates the strategy
are stronger than the demonstrated result.

### [P1] Paper Fig. 4 is not quantitatively reproducible from reported data

The paper does not report numerical \(B\), \(R\), critic node count, critic
centers, initial critic weights, \(\delta_c\), or actuator limits. These
parameters determine sign, scale, and transient direction.

Under this repository's assumptions,

\[
|\hat C|\le\|\hat W_c\|\|\theta_c\|
\le\delta_c\sqrt{15}=387.2983.
\]

Paper Fig. 4 shows approximately
\(0.85/1.4/2.1\times10^8\). The smallest paper level is therefore about
\(2.195\times10^5\) times above the repository's analytic maximum. Using the
observed maximum \(\|\theta_c\|=0.7494\), even the smallest paper curve would
require \(\delta_c\ge1.134\times10^8\), versus the assumed 100.

This does not prove the paper used an invalid \(\delta_c\); it proves that the
published setup omits information required to reconcile its figure with Eq.
(14), and that the repository's current configuration cannot reproduce that
scale.

### [P1] It is unclear whether the paper plots \(C\), \(\hat C\), or another accumulation

Eq. (15) is a future discounted return. With the paper's non-zero initial
errors it must already be positive at \(t=0\). Paper Fig. 4 instead appears to
rise rapidly from zero, which is characteristic of an online estimate
initialized at zero, not the exact future integral. The caption calls it only
"The long-term cost function over time" and does not specify the plotted
producer.

An alternative possibility is that the authors plotted a forward cumulative
past cost rather than Eq. (15). That would explain a monotone rise from zero,
but would be a different mathematical quantity. Author clarification or
original plotting code is required.

### [P2] Critic input notation and repository configuration disagree

The paper writes the ideal/estimated critic basis as \(\theta_c(\eta)\), but
Eq. (19) differentiates it with respect to \(\chi_i\) and multiplies by
\(\upsilon_i=\dot\chi_i\). The repository hard-codes `chi_i` in
`critic_basis.m`. Although `paper_params.m` exposes
`critic_input_mode='chi'`, that flag is not consumed by the critic path; it is
only checked for field presence by `verify_step03_audit.m`. The documented
alternative is therefore not executable.

### [P2] Current critic tests do not validate a value function

- `verify_step34_critic_output` checks only scalar/finite output.
- `verify_step36_bellman_error` checks only finite value and dimensions.
- `verify_step37_critic_update` checks that the implementation equals the same
  literal algebraic expression.
- `verify_step35_utility` correctly checks positive immediate cost, but no test
  connects that property to the sign or numerical accuracy of \(\hat C\).

No oracle compares \(\hat C\) against a backward discounted return, checks
Bellman consistency on a saved trajectory, checks excitation/rank, or rejects
a negative cost-to-go.

## 6. Closed-loop impact

This is not merely a reporting defect. `nn/actor_update.m` injects the critic
value into the actor update through

\[
\tanh\!\left(\hat w_{aij}^T\theta_{aij}+c_a^0\hat C_i\right).
\]

A negative \(\hat C_i\) therefore changes actor weights, the learned dynamics
term, the command, and the physical trajectory. Paper Eq. (35) also sets the
desired ideal cost-to-go to \(C_d=0\); an unconstrained negative estimate is
then numerically "below" that ideal even though no true return can be negative.

## 7. Requested next work

Do not patch the existing plot. First add a diagnostic/value-validation layer:

1. Persist or deterministically recompute `tau_act(t)` and the exact
   instantaneous `r(t)` used by the critic.
2. Compute a backward finite-horizon approximation
   \[
   C_{return}(t_k)\approx
   \sum_{\ell\ge k}e^{-(t_\ell-t_k)/\lambda}r(t_\ell)\Delta t,
   \]
   with an explicit terminal-tail assumption.
3. Plot `C_return`, `Chat`, and their error together, and add an oracle that
   rejects negative `Chat` when it is claimed as a valid cost-to-go.
4. Log `Phi'*theta`, `c_e`, `||Wdot||`, and the basis/input trajectory over the
   first 0.2 s so the sign reversal remains directly reproducible.
5. Wire `critic_input_mode` if the `eta`/`chi` ambiguity is to be tested; keep
   both branches explicitly labeled.
6. Ask the authors/supervisor for the missing \(B,R,m_c,o_i,W_c(0),\delta_c\)
   and confirmation of exactly what was plotted in Fig. 4.

Only after the paper-literal diagnostic is complete should a
positivity-preserving critic be considered. Examples include non-negative
weights with positive RBFs or a positive output map such as softplus. Either
would be a documented experimental correction, not a literal reproduction of
Eqs. (14) and (20), and would require fresh closed-loop validation because
\(\hat C\) feeds the actor.
