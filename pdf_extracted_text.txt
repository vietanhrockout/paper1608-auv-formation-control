H I G H L I G H T S
• To solve an uncertain model and to minimize the future execution cost, an actor-critic neural network reinforcement learning algorithm is developed.
• To ensure the convergence of formation errors and avoid singularity without the use of any switching functions, a novel predefined-time terminal sliding surface is 
designed, thereby avoiding the overestimation issue in fixed-time stability.
• To address the issue of actuator saturation, an adaptive anti-windup compensator is designed to provide real-time compensation for the saturation effects.

A B S T R A C T
In this paper, the cooperative formation problem of multiple autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) is investi­
gated, taking into account disturbances, unknown system models, and input saturation. Based on predefined-time 
stability, reinforcement learning (RL) neural network (NN), and an adaptive method, a novel model-free control 
protocol is designed to ensure that the semi-global formation errors converge to a bounded region of origin. 
This settling time is independent of control parameters, thereby avoiding the overestimation issue. To solve un­
certain model and to minimize the future execution cost, an actor-critic NN RL algorithm is proposed, where 
the actor network is responsible for generating the control law, and the critic network is employed to evaluate 
the execution cost. Moreover, a novel terminal sliding surface is designed to ensure the convergence of forma­
tion errors and avoid singularity without any switching functions. To address the issue of actuator constraints, 
an adaptive anti-windup compensator is designed to provide real-time compensation for the saturation effects. 
The semi-global predefined-time stability of the closed-loop system is rigorously proven using Lyapunov theory, 
demonstrating that all AUVs reach their desired positions. Finally, simulation results validate the effectiveness 
and practical engineering value of the proposed method.
1 . Introduction
With the depletion of terrestrial resources, marine resources have 
become a crucial asset for the sustainable development of human soci­
ety. The exploitation of marine resources and the associated competition 
have become strategic priorities. Due to the unique challenges of the 
underwater environment [ 1], humans cannot directly and efficiently 
perform tasks, necessitating the use of underwater vehicles to assist in 
ocean exploration. Despite their high costs and limited battery life, au­
tonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) [2] offer significant advantages in 
terms of payload capacity, maneuverability, and operational depth, gar­
nering increasing attention from scientists and engineers. However, as 
the complexity of tasks increases, a single AUV system can no longer

meet mission requirements, making it nearly impossible to achieve sat­

has garnered significant attention over the past few decades. Recently, 
multi-AUV systems have gradually gained the capability to operate con­
tinuously in complex environments, becoming efficient and reliable 
underwater exploration systems. Thus, individual units can exchange 
sensor data, carry sonar arrays, and coordinate with each other to form 
an integrated system, thereby creating a reconfigurable sensor array 
capable of efficient terrain exploration and data collection.
In fact, developing high-performance formation controllers for a 
group of AUVs is challenging due to the complex dynamics, which 
include strong coupling, high nonlinearity, and system constraints. 
Additionally, AUVs operating are subject to ocean current disturbances 
and hydrodynamic effects, leading to model uncertainties. Sliding mode 
control (SMC) [4,5] is a nonlinear strategy known for its fast response 
and strong robustness. It is insensitive to disturbances and particularly 
well-suited for handling the dynamic of robots, having already achieved 
many notable results [6–8]. Moreover, achieving rapid convergence of 
multi-AUV formations is a critical performance indicator for control 
systems. Most previous AUVs formation control strategies are asymp­
totically stable, meaning the convergence speed follows an exponential 
form. To address this limitation, researchers proposed finite-time con­
trol [9], which ensures that the system states converge to the equilibrium 
point within a finite time. However, the convergence time of finite-time 
stability grows unboundedly with increasing initial values, significantly 
reducing the system’s performance. In practical engineering applica­
tions, the initial information is often difficult to obtain, further limiting 
the applicability of these algorithms. To solve this issue, fixed-time the­
ory [4,10] was introduced. It enhances the convergence speed of the 
system and eliminates the dependency on initial states. Its the critical 
drawback is that the convergence time depends on the controller pa­
rameters. In most cases, the settling time is overestimated, leading to 
an imprecise stabilization time. For systems that require strict conver­
gence time guarantees, such as AUVs, further improvements in controller 
design based on fixed-time theory are necessary.
To address the aforementioned issues, the predefined-time stability 
theory [11,12] has been proposed. Its settling time is predetermined 
by the designer and thus independent of the initial values and con­
troller parameters. Predefined-time stability has attracted widespread 
research attention [
13]. To stabilize a nonlinear system with actuator 
failure and disturbance, a predefined-time strategy is developed in [14] 
by using an event-triggered rule and NN. An adaptive predefined-time 
protocol is investigated in [15] based on fuzzy logic and adaptive back­
stepping methods for synchronous motor systems with unpredictable 
states. To achieve trajectory tracking of AUVs, a predefined-time proto­
col with prescribed performance is studied in [16] to ensure the desired 
steady-state performance. Conventional terminal SMC for achieving 
predefined-time convergence often introduces singularity problems [13,
17], which pose significant challenges to practical algorithm implemen­
tation. Therefore, singularity avoidance becomes imperative. Feng et al. 
[18] proposed a nonsingular surface, laying the foundation for nonsin­
gular SMC. Building upon this work, reference [19] introduced a novel 
nonsingular terminal sliding surface incorporating an 𝑎𝑟𝑐𝑡𝑎𝑛 function to 
achieve trajectory tracking for uncertain robotic systems. Study [20] de­
veloped a NN-based nonsingular terminal SMC scheme for robots, with 
its key innovation being the design of a segmented sliding surface that 
effectively circumvents the singularity issues.
The aforementioned algorithms rely heavily or partially on the robot 
model [13–16,19]. Formation control [21,22] algorithms can meet the 
allowable error requirements of formation control under certain con­
ditions, but they struggle to accurately estimate system models in 
real-world environments, especially in rapidly changing underwater 
conditions. As the number of AUVs increases [ 23,24], the parameters 
of the formation system become more coupled, and the complexity of 
the control algorithm increases accordingly [25]. In unknown environ­
ments, model parameters are influenced by external conditions, and 
the uncertainty of these parameters further increases the difficulty of 
control. This research struggles to meet the real-time requirements of 
formation control. To overcome the dependence on model parameters, 
therefore, researchers have proposed RL algorithm [ 26–29]. It is well 
known that value-based methods predict the value of actions through 
NNs, with their objective function being the TD error; policy-based 
methods directly predict the probability distribution of actions via NNs 
based on the input state, i.e., they optimize the policy directly, with their 
objective function being the cross-entropy weighted by cumulative re­
wards. Actor-Critic combines the two approaches, meaning that it is a 
method that is both value-based and policy-based, and it has achieved 
remarkable results in robot control. To control a flexible manipulator 
and suppress the vibration, two RBF NNs in [ 30] including the actor 
network and critic network are employed to develop a control algo­
rithm. Reference [31] integrated SMC with actor-critic NNs to develop 
a model-free control law for robotic manipulator trajectory tracking, 
while simultaneously minimizing the execution cost. In [ 32] a novel 
online decision-making method adopting the soft actor-critic technique 
to achieve underwater cooperative pursuit of multi-AUVs is proposed.
In practical engineering applications, the thrust output of an AUV’s 
propulsion system is subject to distinct physical limits, making input 
saturation an inevitable phenomenon [ 24]. When the force required 
for a formation task exceeds these limits, the actual input of the AUV 
will be constrained at the saturation boundary. This effect can severely 
disrupt the intended structure of the control law, potentially leading 
to issues such as stability degradation, performance deterioration, and 
intensified chattering. Therefore, in AUV formation control, input satu­
ration is no longer a negligible secondary factor but a core constraint 
that must be addressed directly. Current mature approaches include 
designing adaptive compensators [33] to handle this constraint or em­
ploying NNs [34], while more advanced methods leverage RL to derive 
optimal input forces. Precisely because it achieves optimal input and 
avoids the overestimation typical of traditional methods, RL can effec­
tively balance the required convergence characteristics with saturation 
constraints. Among these, critic-actor networks [ 31] and approximate 
dynamic programming [35–37] are widely applied, as they enable the 
design of cost functions to achieve optimal switching control.
Inspired by the above analysis, addressing the issue of predefined-
time formation tracking for AUVs with disturbances, uncertainties, 
actuator saturation, and unknown models remains a challenge. To elim­
inate the dependence of the formation controller on the AUV model 
while achieving the minimum cost-to-go, two actor-critic RL NNs are 
proposed. To address actuator saturation, an adaptive compensator 
is implemented to dynamically mitigate saturation effects and ensure 
stable performance. The specific contributions of this paper are as 
follows:
1. Unlike constrained methods [ 4,38,39], our approach allows the 
settling time of closed-loop systems to be predefined to meet 
specific convergence requirements. This paper designs a termi­
nal sliding surface and controller without relying on switching 
or auxiliary functions, which achieve predefined-time stability 
and reduce computational complexity. Compared to previous re­
search [13,17], the proposed predefined-time sliding avoids both 
singularity and discontinuity problems.
2. Different from works that require the system model as a prior 
knowledge [ 4,33,35,40,41], the RL framework is employed, 
where the actor network generates optimal control inputs and 
the critic network evaluates the execution cost, minimizing fu­
ture control effort without relying on system information, which 
provides strong robustness for the cooperative formation of AUVs 
in complex and uncertain underwater environments.
3. While NN controllers have achieved numerous results [ 34,42], 
they generally neglect cost functions and thus fail to achieve 
optimal control. The proposed method employs an actor-critic

4 . Model-free control protocol
The above protocol ( 25) does not account for actuator saturation. 
To address the actuator saturation issue in AUVs, this paper designs 
an adaptive law to compensate for saturation faults, ensuring that each 
AUVs can achieve the desired motion state even in the event of thrust 
saturation. The designed adaptive law is
̇ 𝜛𝑖 = −𝑘 2𝜛𝑖 − sig𝜍1 (𝜎3sig𝜍2 (𝜛𝑖) +𝜎 4sig𝜍3 (𝜛𝑖)) +𝑠𝑖 + Δ𝜏𝑖 (30)
where 𝑘2 denotes a positive definite matrices. Although large initial 
errors would require excessively high control torques to stabilize the 
AUVs, which contradicts their actual constraints and prevents global sta­
bility. This study emphasizes that the RL NN generates control inputs by 
minimizing a cost function. This ensures optimal torque when the cost 
is minimized, effectively mitigating the high-frequency chattering inher­
ent in traditional SMC while optimally reducing input force. Thus, our 
strategy provides a theoretical foundation for studying multi-AUVs for­
mation under input constraints, achieving semi-global predefined-time 
stability of formation errors.

7 . Conclusion
This study has addressed the formation problem for multiple AUVs 
under disturbances, unknown dynamics, and input constraints. A novel 
model-free control framework was developed, integrating predefined-
time theory, RL, and adaptive compensation to ensure robust and 
precise formation tracking. Unlike fixed-time approaches, the proposed 
method guarantees semi-global convergence within a predefined time, 
eliminating the overestimation issue while maintaining stability. To 
handle system uncertainties and optimize control performance, an RL 
strategy was implemented, utilizing actor-critic NNs. The actor net­
work generated control inputs, while the critic network evaluated 
long-term costs, enhancing adaptability in unknown environments. 
Furthermore, a singularity-free sliding surface was introduced to ensure 
rapid and smooth convergence without relying on switching functions. 
Additionally, an adaptive anti-windup compensator effectively miti­
gated actuator saturation effects, improving control reliability. The 
predefined-time stability of the closed-loop system was rigorously estab­
lished via Lyapunov analysis. Extensive numerical simulations demon­
strated the efficacy and robustness of the proposed approach, showcas­
ing its potential for practical applications in underwater multi-vehicle 
systems. Future work will focus on developing simplified NN archi­
tectures and more efficient learning rules to reduce the computational 
burden and enhance the method’s practicality for real-time applications 
with stricter resource constraints.
